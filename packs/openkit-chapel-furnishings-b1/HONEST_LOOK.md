# Look and limits

- The masonry and the timber are chamfered and regular, with little wear, so the set looks cleaner than a real chapel.
- The piscina is a kit-sized stone wall with a mid-height basin niche about 0.52 m wide and 0.60 m tall above a visible lip at about 1.0 m, a stone bowl, a flat water disc and an iron drain on the lip. It does not reach the floor, and it is not a doorway or a window. It reads as a wall basin from a 50 mm camera at 13 m. This is deliberate: the niche is a recess, not a passage, and the solid wall collider fills it. Up close the bowl is a set of boxes and the water is a flat disc, not a meniscus.
- The ambry is a kit-sized stone wall with a mid-height recessed wooden cupboard, iron hinges and an iron hasp. It does not reach the floor, so it is not a room door, and it has a full door leaf rather than a slot, so it is not a letter box. It reads as a wall cupboard from 13 m. This is deliberate: the door leaf does not swing and the solid wall collider fills the niche.
- The pulpit is an enclosed raised box on four posts, with a short stair, and a sloped book board. It reads as a pulpit from 13 m, not as a lectern or a watchtower. The space under the deck between the posts is visually open and is a real collider gap. The stair risers are solid. Up close the enclosure and the stair are simple boxes.
- The choir stall is a single high-backed seat with cheek wings and a crest rail. It reads as a stall from 13 m, not as a backless bench or a long pew. The single seat can still recall a high-backed chair; the wings and the high back are what mark it as a choir stall. The space under the seat is visually open and is a real collider gap.
- The prie-dieu is a low kneeling step, two slim uprights, and a thin sloped book rest at about 0.80 m, with two small iron pins under it. The front and the underside under the rest are visually open and are real collider gaps. It reads as a kneeler from 13 m, not as a closed box or a doghouse, and not as a chair or a lectern.
- The credence is a small side table with a lower shelf and two lidded bronze cruets. It reads as a side table from 13 m, not as an altar or a dining table. Up close the cruets are stacked cylinders with simple lids.
- The pulpit and choir-stall iron studs sit inside the timber and do not show; the prie-dieu pins show only as small dots under the book rest.
- Collision notes describe the reference shells, which are not included.
- An independent GLB parse reports coplanar same-facing overlaps on all six pieces, most of them on the coursed masonry of the two walls, and unpaired edges on the piscina, the ambry, the pulpit and the prie-dieu where parts of different material slots meet. Official Blender re-import integrity was closed on every piece.
- All observations above come from Blender Workbench previews, including a 50 mm perspective camera at 13 m. Nothing has been checked under game-engine lighting, against a character, or with a camera-fade shader.
