# Look and limits

- The timber is chamfered and regular, with little wear, so the frames look cleaner than a real yard.
- The battering ram is a 2.48 m log on two trestles, with iron hoops and a capped striking head. It reads clearly from a 50 mm camera at 13 m. The space under the log between the trestles, and the space between each trestle's legs, is a real collider gap.
- The ballista is a compact scorpion-scale engine (about 1.37 × 1.43 m on the ground, 0.86 m tall) with a two-segment front prod and a bowstring across the tips. The space between the side frames is visually open and is a real collider gap. The prod, string, winch, tiller and a single bolt on the rail still read at 13 m. Up close the limbs are simple squared timbers, not curved springs.
- Ballista: from the 3/4 view the left prod arm hides behind the frame, so the bow can look one-sided.
- The pavise is a tall pointed standing shield with a plain iron boss, a shoe, two battens and a rear leaning prop. This is deliberate: the face is a solid panel; nothing passes through the board. The space under the leaning prop is a real gap. The board face is plain timber. It reads as a shield from 13 m.
- The mantlet is a wider, shorter wheeled screen with a 6 cm viewing slit between the leaves. The slit is visually open and is a real collider gap, as is the space under the screen between the wheels. The slit still reads at 13 m. An independent GLB parse still reports a few coplanar same-facing overlaps where separate timber parts meet; official Blender re-import integrity passed.
- The stone shot is three faceted balls of different sizes sitting on the ground, with small gaps between them. Each stone has its own collider; the spaces between them are real gaps. The facets are obvious up close.
- The bolt rack is a low timber stand holding four horizontal bolts with iron heads. This is deliberate: one reference envelope covers the set; the openings between the bolts are visual only.
- Collision notes describe the reference shells, which are not included.
- All observations above come from Blender Workbench previews, including a 50 mm perspective camera at 13 m. Nothing has been checked under game-engine lighting, against a character, or with a camera-fade shader.
