# Look and limits

- The masonry, timber and leadwork are chamfered and regular, with little wear, so the walk looks cleaner than a real cloister.
- The arcade is a kit-sized stone wall with slim piers, imposts at about 2.05 m, and an open pointed two-centred arch down to the floor. A full-thickness ring of voussoirs and a keystone follows the arch; two iron cramps sit on the cap. The opening is visually open and is a real collider gap. It reads as an arcade bay from a 50 mm camera at 13 m. Up close the voussoirs are faceted wedges, not a smooth curve.
- The floor tile is a 1.5 m cell with a mortar bed, a border, a 2-by-2 inner field and a flush iron diamond in a mortar well at the centre. This is deliberate: the solid cell collider fills the joints and the inlay. Up close the diamond is a flat four-sided plate.
- The bench is a backless stone slab on two solid ends, with two iron pins on the seat. The space under the slab is visually open and is a real collider gap. Up close the ends and the slab are simple chamfered boxes.
- The hopper is a wall-mounted lead funnel: a flared open box against a back plane, iron brackets and wall pads, an iron downpipe, and a shoe that discharges into the walk. It reads as a rain hopper from 13 m, not as a lamp post. Up close the flare is a set of sloped boxes, and the mouth is a simple rectangular opening. This is deliberate: the funnel mouth is a shallow recess and is filled by the hopper box.
- The alms box is a timber chest on a post and plinth, with an iron strap around the chest, a hasp and staple on one side, and an iron coin-slot plate on the lid. This is deliberate: the slot is a shallow recess in the plate and is filled. From some angles the chest on a post can still recall a roadside letter box; the strap, hasp and slotted plate are there to mark it as an alms box.
- The trough is a long low stone basin with a flat water disc below the rim and two iron staples on the outer rim. The mouth above the water is visually open and is a real collider gap. Up close the water is a flat disc, not a meniscus.
- Collision notes describe the reference shells, which are not included.
- An independent GLB parse reports coplanar same-facing overlaps on the arcade (coursed piers, spandrels and voussoirs), on the hopper and on the alms box, and a few unpaired edges on the hopper and the trough where parts meet across material slots. Official Blender re-import integrity was closed on every piece, with same-facing coplanar overlaps of 0 on the floor tile, the bench and the trough.
- All observations above come from Blender Workbench previews, including a 50 mm perspective camera at 13 m. Nothing has been checked under game-engine lighting, against a character, or with a camera-fade shader.
